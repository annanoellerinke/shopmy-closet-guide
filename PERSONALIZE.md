# Make the closet yours

Start with your own references, clothes, and shelves. You don't need Anna's products, wood finish, handwriting, or number of categories.

## Bring a few inspiration photos

A real wardrobe, your own clothing rail, a vintage cabinet, a store interior, or an editorial photograph can all work. Tell your agent what you like and what you don't.

For example: “I like the reflection on the wood,” “the clothes should overlap but still be recognizable,” “this feels too perfectly arranged,” or “I want the room to feel lived in.”

Your own photograph is an option too. Keep the pieces and labels large enough to use on a phone. If you use generated imagery, check the shapes, logos, textures, and product colors before approving it.

## Choose the setting and objects

| Choice | Some possibilities |
| --- | --- |
| Closet | Vintage wardrobe, built-in cabinetry, open clothing rail, or a small boutique. |
| Materials | Dark wood, pale wood, painted cabinetry, brushed metal, or warm plaster. |
| Arrangement | Overlapping hangers, folded stacks, shoes below, and small objects on a shelf. |
| Labels | Handwritten arrows, tags, printed names, or a mix. |
| Heading | Your name and shop name, with a short instruction such as “Tap a piece to shop.” |
| Destinations | Your exact ShopMy shelves, or specific product links if you explicitly choose them. |

The objects represent categories; you don't have to display every product in every shelf. More than one object can open the same shelf. In Anna's example, a cap and both bags open Accessories, and the camera and laptop open Work Things.

## Decide what happens on a wider screen

A tall closet photo won't naturally fill a wide desktop screen without getting larger, cropping, or leaving space on the sides. Ask to see the actual options before choosing.

| Option | What it looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Same centered photo | The original image sits against a background color you choose. |
| Wider desktop scene | Added surroundings or cabinet panels frame the center. The phone uses a closer image. |
| Full-width portrait | The photo fills the browser width, which means more vertical scrolling on desktop. |

Anna's final version uses **narrow built-in side panels on desktop** and the **original portrait closet on phones**. That is an example, not a requirement. Ask for a desktop preview and a phone preview before approving yours.

If the two images have different compositions, each needs its own clickable outlines. You should not need to draw these yourself; ask your coding agent to handle them.

## Fill in your shelf map

Copy this for each shelf:

```text
Shelf name:
Exact ShopMy shelf URL:
Object or objects that should represent it:
Product name and color, if important:
Product photo, personal photo, or retailer reference:
Where it should sit:
Exact label wording:
Anything to avoid:
```

Keep the **ShopMy destination** separate from the **product reference**. A retailer page can identify a pair of boots while tapping the boots opens your Shoes shelf.

## Feedback you can copy

> Show me two compositions before building the website.

> Make the clothes feel casually arranged, with some turned sideways and partly hidden.

> The textures feel artificial. Match the irregular reflections and fabric detail in my reference.

> Make the desktop panels narrower. Keep the phone version as it is.

> This is the approved image. Don't regenerate it to add links or change a font. Put text and interactions on separate layers wherever possible.

> Show me how every piece and label works on my phone, and include a readable list of all shelves.
